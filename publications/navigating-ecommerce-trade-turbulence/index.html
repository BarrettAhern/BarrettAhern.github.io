Navigating eCommerce Trade Turbulence in 2025

Practical ways for brands to protect growth and margin while tariffs keep changing.

Barrett Ahern and Nathan Lomax · Dash · Published 27 Jun 2025 · Updated 5 Nov 2026
This is a summary. The full article is on Dash's blog.

Tariffs squeeze margins, but the article argues the bigger threat is uncertainty. It delays projects, disrupts supply chains and weakens consumer confidence as much as higher costs do.

Waiting for clarity is not a plan. The piece sets out ways to build the ability to adapt, through sourcing, pricing, scenario planning and technology.

Since publication

Supreme Court ruling and a 10% baseline tariff

Feb 2026

Dash added an update to the original article. It notes a Supreme Court ruling that cancelled some tariffs, and a 10% baseline tariff from 24 February.

What the article recommends

  1. Plan for sudden change. Policy can halt a supply chain overnight, and uncertainty alone can hurt demand.
  2. Add supply flexibility. Nearshoring, bonded warehouses and foreign trade zones help, though realigning a supply chain can take 12 to 18 months.
  3. Price SKU by SKU. Raise prices on hero products and hold entry-level lines steady.
  4. Model several tariff levels. Prepare the operational response and the customer message for each.
  5. Make content work harder. Reuse what exists and give stockists instant access to assets.
  6. Use technology to reduce duty surprises. That covers tariff estimates, HS code classification and checkout changes, plus product data that is ready for agentic commerce.

Who it is for

Brand and operations leads managing margin under changing trade policy.
