SEO in an AI-First Search Landscape

Why brands should manage how AI platforms describe them, not only how much traffic those platforms send.

Barrett Ahern · Vervaunt · Published Mar 2026 · Updated 5 Nov 2026
This is a summary. The full commentary is on Vervaunt's site.

ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini now shape how people research and choose products. The commentary treats generative engine optimisation (GEO) as largely a rebrand of SEO, but argues that the surfaces, the mechanics and the goals have changed.

AI platforms often answer a question without sending anyone to the brand's site, and sometimes get the answer wrong. Traffic alone stops being a reliable measure of success, so the practical job becomes looking after the facts these platforms draw on: product data, external reputation and what happens after the sale.

What the commentary recommends

  1. Structure product data for comparison. Record the attributes shoppers actually compare, and keep details such as size guides in plain HTML rather than widgets or PDFs.
  2. Audit what AI platforms say about you. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews about your brand, products and competitors, log the gaps and the sources cited, and repeat it.
  3. Keep outside sources accurate. Wikipedia, Reddit, Google Business Profile and review sites carry weight with AI platforms, and they cannot be corrected directly.
  4. Track leading indicators. Branded search volume and impressions can move while traffic stays flat, even though attribution is not yet precise.
  5. Turn AI discovery into owned relationships. Loyalty, service and post-purchase engagement bring customers back to your own site.
  6. Coordinate organic and paid. Specific identifiers such as SKUs and model numbers face little competition and are often cited in AI recommendations.

Who it is for

Digital and eCommerce leads deciding what to change in search and content plans.
